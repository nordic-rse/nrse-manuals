# Conference budgeting

The first thing to do when starting to plan a conference is to get an overview of the budget. The conference is generally planned to break even: the association's reserves can cover a shortfall, but shouldn't be planned in.

## How to budget

- Keep the budget updated at all times, and make clearly visible what is an estimate and what is the real cost, and in which currency.
- Budget for a few attendance scenarios (low, expected, high). Room and catering costs scale with the number of participants, and so does the ticket price you need to break even.
- Note the cancellation terms and payment deadlines for the venue and catering. These decide when you're committed to a cost.

The [Finance chair](roles.md#finance-chair) (or the main organizer, for a smaller conference) keeps the budget up to date, in close contact with the board's treasurer.

In general, the board oversees the budget. It can however give that task to the conference organizers so that they can make decisions quickly. 

## Things to consider

### Income

- Ticket prices: regular and reduced (e.g. students), early bird, day tickets?
- Funding and sponsorship, see [funding.md](funding.md)
- In-kind contributions, e.g. a free venue or AV support — not cash, but they remove whole cost lines
- Paid extras, e.g. the conference dinner as an optional add-on

### Costs

- Free tickets (speakers, organizers, sponsors, students)
- Room costs
- Catering costs
- Dinner costs 
- Social events and excursions
- AV and streaming: equipment hire, venue tech support (see [av.md](av.md)), if needed
- Costs for security (if needed)
- Printing: name tags, banner, signage, flyers
- Participant goodies 
- Speaker and organizer gifts
- Travel cost and honorarium for keynote speakers
- Travel grants for participants, if offered
- Payment fees: ticketing platform and card fees, currency conversion
